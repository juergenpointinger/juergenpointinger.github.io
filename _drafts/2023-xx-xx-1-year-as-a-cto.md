As I reflect on my first year as a Chief Technology Officer at Untis GmbH, I can't help but feel both humbled and proud of our journey. It has been a rollercoaster ride, full of challenges and opportunities for growth. In this blog post, I will share some personal insights and vulnerability while discussing what I have learned as an agile leader in a company developing solutions to make your everyday life at school easier.

## Company Strategy and Team Structure

We have already started to promote self-organization in different areas of the organization and to transform our product development teams into autonomous teams. Which is quite challenging at times. We worked on breaking down silos within and outside the product engineering teams by improving transparency, team metrics, and quality.

We dealt with technical, historical management and enterprise debt, which meant we still had a long way to go. To address these challenges, we changed the company strategy, and around half of all employees were involved in creating it. We also changed teams to more cross-functional teams, which has helped to break down silos and improve collaboration. Yes, you heard right! It was a great experience and we will definitely do it again.

We already had communities for different areas, such as DevOps or Backend, but we additionally introduced an architecture forum to improve decision-making and transparency. It was also important to us to promote sharing and joint learning.

## Effective Communication

My professional background taught me that effective communication is critical in my role. At Untis, it was critical to make sure everyone understood what was expected of them, and was able to ask questions and provide feedback as needed. 

It's important to us to have the conversations with everyone in the company on an equal footing, which also means, above all, that you have to listen and there should be no hierarchy. By listening to feedback from stakeholders and the team, we were able to better understand the different perspectives and make better decisions.

## Continuous Improvement

As an agile leader, I also learned the importance of continuous improvement. At Untis, we use agile methodologies and DevOps practices to develop our products, and this requires a constant focus on feedback and adaptation. I quickly realized that this approach applies not only to the development process but also to leadership. Seeking feedback from product engineering teams and peers and using it to improve my leadership skills continually has been crucial.

Continuous improvement meant taking a critical look at my leadership style and identifying areas for improvement. I focused on developing my communication skills, learning to delegate effectively, and building a stronger rapport with the teams.

We trained 1/3 of our employees in agile fundamentals, and we improved technical excellence with specific workshops and code retreats. However, Continuous Integration and Delivery is still something we need to work and focus on, as well as improve and extend our test automation practices.

In addition, to learn together, we have also introduced a Learning & Achievements event where we meet every month to share our experiences. The event has become a highlight for many.

## People-Centric Culture

Untis has a people-centric culture, and we worked on things like transparency from the management, implemented a People Guide role, and had a focus on hiring the right people (Raising the bar principle). We created a transparent salary model and set up a Work-From-Anywhere possibility as a benefit for our employees. This culture has helped us to attract and retain top talent and has enabled us to create an environment where everyone feels valued and respected.

## Vulnerability as a Leader

I also learned the importance of being vulnerable as a leader - a point I've always struggled with in the past. But with Untis, the colleagues and the culture, it's very easy. I can readily admit that I don't have all the answers, and asked the teams for input. This creates a culture of openness where hopefully everyone feels comfortable sharing their thoughts and ideas. This leads to better collaboration and, in the end, innovation.

As an agile leader, it's important to recognize that we don't have all the answers and that the best ideas can come from anyone in the organization. When we create an environment where everyone feels empowered to share their thoughts and ideas, we create a more innovative and successful company.

I still make mistakes here in particular, which could sometimes be related to Imposter Syndrome, where I don't believe in my own accomplishments and successes. But I'm working on it and I'm getting better at it.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, my first year as a CTO at Untis has been a valuable learning experience. I learned that involving employees in creating the company strategy and changing teams to more cross-functional teams can help break down silos and improve collaboration. Effective communication, empowering the team, continuous improvement, and vulnerability as a leader are essential traits for agile leadership. A people-centric culture that focuses on transparency, hiring the right people, and providing benefits like Work-From-Anywhere can attract and retain top talent. By continuing to focus on these areas, we can create a culture of excellence that drives innovation and success.
